A late symbol landing on the next one

Each symbol is a stretch of carrier held at one amplitude and one phase. An echo arrives a little later, so part of one symbol's carrier is still arriving while the next symbol is being received. Watch what the sum looks like inside that second slot.

What the receiver ends up with

Each symbol is one point of the 16-QAM constellation from session 2 part 3. The receiver measures the amplitude and the phase of whatever is in the slot, so when a piece of the previous symbol is still there, it measures the wrong point.

Grey dots are the 16 points that can be sent. Each one splits into a small group of 16, one per possible preceding symbol. The orange group belongs to symbol 2 of the drawing above, and the ring marks the one case drawn there: symbol 2 received after symbol 1. The faint grid is the decision boundary, so a group that reaches it is a group the receiver can read as the wrong symbol.

There is no randomness on this page. All 256 combinations are computed, so the picture is complete rather than a sample.

Two things the drawing simplifies, said out loud

  • The carrier is drawn at 20 MHz so that individual cycles are visible. A real 2.4 GHz carrier fits roughly 1200 cycles into a 500 ns symbol, which cannot be drawn.
  • Both the delay and the symbol length move in steps of 50 ns, which is exactly one cycle of that drawn carrier. So the echo always arrives in phase with the direct path, and a symbol always holds a whole number of cycles. Whether two copies add or cancel is the question of the previous slide, about path differences of λ/2; this page is about the much longer delays that make one symbol reach into the next.
  • The receiver is assumed to have corrected for the gain of the current symbol's own two copies. What is left on the constellation is interference from the previous symbol and nothing else.

Three things worth trying

  • Set the delay to zero. The echo lands entirely inside its own slot, the groups collapse back to points, and nothing is damaged. A strong echo is not automatically a harmful one.
  • Hold the delay at 200 ns and raise the symbol rate. The environment has not changed and the echo has not changed, yet the groups keep growing. This is the slide Short symbols suffer more.
  • Push the delay to 400 ns and the symbol length down to 450 ns. The orange group now reaches the decision boundary, so the receiver can read that symbol as one of its neighbours. That is the point at which the room, not the bandwidth, is what limits the symbol rate.
