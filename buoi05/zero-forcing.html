A MIMO link end to end, with zero forcing

Three transmit antennas, three receive antennas, three QPSK streams at once. Follow one symbol from the three constellations it starts on, through the room, through the inverse of H, to the three constellations it ends on. Then make the room harder and watch where it breaks.

The three streams carry the same three symbols the whole time, 00, 11 and 10, so the only thing changing from one frame to the next is the noise. The middle row is the one students find surprising: no receive antenna sees a QPSK constellation. Each one hears a mixture of all three streams, so its points land anywhere. The grid only comes back in the bottom row, after the receiver multiplies by H−1.

Things worth trying

Leave the noise at 20 dB and push how alike two paths are up slowly. The middle row barely changes: the antennas are receiving just as strong a signal as before. The bottom row falls apart. Nothing happened in the room to the signal strength; what happened is that H became hard to invert, and the receiver did the inverting.

Now put the slider back to 0 and lower the noise slider instead. Both rows get worse together, which is the ordinary behaviour students expect. The point of the first experiment is that the ill-conditioned case does not behave that way.

Watch the two SNR numbers. The first is what the antennas see and the slider sets it directly. The second is what is left on the worst stream after H−1. With a good room the two are close; with two nearly identical paths the second collapses while the first does not move.
