FDM versus TDM

Session 2, Part 3. Three lanes at a steady speed, or one lane taken in turns at three times the speed.

Back to the INT3303 simulation index

Think of the medium as a stretch of road. FDM paints it into three lanes and gives one to each source: everybody drives at the same time, each in a lane of its own. TDM leaves one wide lane and lets the sources take turns, but a source that gets the whole road can drive three times as fast. The same traffic gets through either way.

What to look for
  1. Count the cars that reach the far end in one cycle. It is the same number in both schemes, because the road carries the same total traffic.
  2. In FDM every car moves slowly but never stops. In TDM a car waits, then crosses at full speed. Same throughput, different delay.
  3. The dashed strips between the FDM lanes are guard bands. They carry nothing, and they are the price of dividing along frequency.
  4. Switch B off. In FDM lane B stays empty the whole time. In TDM B's turn still comes and the road sits idle for that turn. Neither can hand the gap to A or C.
Built for INT3303. Both schemes, and the point that each wastes capacity to keep the implementation simple, follow Beard & Stallings, section 2.5.
